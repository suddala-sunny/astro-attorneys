# ASTRO ATTORNEYS — Business & Website Blueprint

## 1. Brand and positioning

**Brand:** Astro Attorneys

**Practice:** Vedic Astrology and Tamil Jothidam  
**Experience statement:** 25 years of experience in astrology (confirm the exact public wording and supporting background before publication).  
**Base:** Tamil Nadu roots; online operations from Bengaluru, Karnataka (as shown on the website; a physical office address is to be announced)  
**Audience:** Clients across India and internationally

**Consultation modes:** Phone, video, in-person by appointment and WhatsApp.

**Suggested tagline:** Ancient Wisdom. Personalised Guidance.

**Positioning statement:** A personal Vedic astrology consultancy rooted in Tamil tradition, offering respectful, confidential consultations in five languages. The service helps clients understand traditional astrological interpretations and reflect on their questions; it does not promise or guarantee outcomes.

### Brand-name caution
“Attorneys” is commonly understood to mean legal professionals. Before investing in signage, domain names or advertising, assess possible customer confusion and obtain professional advice on trademark/business-name clearance. This blueprint retains the requested name but does not establish that it is legally available or suitable.

## 2. Business model

Start as a founder-led consultancy. Keep the first version simple and avoid building an expensive marketplace or automated prediction engine before validating demand.

### Core revenue streams
1. One-to-one birth-chart / Jathagam consultations.
2. Marriage horoscope matching / Porutham consultations.
3. Career and work-related astrological consultations.
4. Family and relationship consultations.
5. Muhurtham / auspicious timing consultations.
6. Written Jathagam or consultation summaries, if Abirami offers and can fulfil them consistently.

These are proposed service categories, not confirmed offerings. Remove or amend any service Abirami does not personally practise.

### Pricing architecture (do not publish prices until approved)
Use a clear fee card with:
- Introductory enquiry: free, limited to a short administrative conversation (not a free reading), if desired.
- Standard consultation: fixed duration and fee.
- Extended consultation: longer duration and fee.
- Written report: separately priced with a stated delivery timeline.
- Follow-up: defined duration, eligibility window and fee.
- In-person consultation: fee and location confirmed before booking.

Show currency clearly (INR for domestic clients; an explicitly stated currency and payment process for international clients). State whether taxes, payment-provider fees or currency conversion charges apply. Do not invent a price or claim tax treatment without advice from a qualified accountant.

## 3. Client journey and operating workflow

1. Discovery: visitor arrives from search, referral, social media or a shared website link.
2. Enquiry: visitor submits name, broad topic, preferred language and preferred contact mode. Do not ask for full birth details on the public enquiry form.
3. Human response: confirm that the requested service is offered, language availability, location/time zone, duration and fee.
4. Consent and booking: share privacy notice, cancellation/rescheduling policy and payment instructions; obtain affirmative agreement.
5. Secure intake: collect only the birth details needed for the selected service through a controlled channel; explain why they are needed and who can access them.
6. Consultation: conduct the session privately; avoid guarantees, fear-based pressure or claims that clients must buy remedies.
7. Follow-up: provide agreed notes/report only if included; invite voluntary feedback without conditioning service on a positive review.
8. Retention/deletion: retain only records needed for service, accounting and legal obligations; define a retention schedule and deletion process.

## 6. Operations and trust standards

- Use one dedicated business phone/WhatsApp number if possible.
- Publish working hours and response-time expectations.
- Maintain a booking register with client consent and minimum necessary data.
- Keep client birth information and consultation notes confidential; do not share screenshots or testimonials without explicit permission.
- Use only genuine testimonials with the client's informed permission; never fabricate reviews or imply guaranteed results.
- Explain the exact scope and fee before payment.
- Publish a written cancellation, rescheduling and refund policy.
- Keep a simple income/expense ledger and issue appropriate receipts.
- Establish a process for complaints and corrections.
- Do not make medical, mental-health, legal or financial diagnoses or replace qualified professional advice.
- Do not use fear-based upselling, threats, guaranteed marriage/job/wealth claims or pressure to purchase gemstones/rituals.

## 7. Privacy and legal/compliance checklist (India)

This is a practical checklist, not legal or tax advice. Have an Indian lawyer and chartered accountant review the final business setup and policies before launch.

- Decide the legal form: individual proprietorship may be simplest for a solo launch, but confirm suitability with a CA/lawyer. Consider a company/LLP only if ownership, liability, investment or growth needs justify it.
- Check business-name and trademark availability before committing to the brand.
- Confirm local trade/shop establishment requirements, if applicable to the chosen premises and state.
- Ask a CA to determine GST registration, invoicing and treatment of domestic and export-of-service revenue. Do not assume a turnover threshold alone resolves registration obligations.
- Use clear prices, service descriptions, payment terms and cancellation/refund terms.
- Do not publish misleading advertisements, unverifiable credentials, fabricated testimonials or guaranteed outcomes.
- Publish a privacy notice describing data collected, purpose, sharing, retention, security, contact for privacy requests and deletion process.
- Birth date, birth time, birthplace, phone number, messages and consultation notes should be treated as personal information. Collect only what is needed, with clear notice and consent where applicable.
- Restrict access to client records, use strong passwords and multi-factor authentication, secure devices, and avoid sending birth details into public AI tools or unsecured forms.
- Review the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 and DPDP Rules, 2025 commencement schedule with counsel; obligations have phased commencement dates.
- For international clients, check cross-border privacy, consumer, tax and payment requirements for the countries actually served.

## 8. Marketing plan

### First 90 days
**Weeks 1–2:** confirm brand clearance, service list, founder bio, location, contact details, fees, working hours and policies.  
**Weeks 3–4:** publish the website; create Google Business Profile only if eligible for the real business setup; set up WhatsApp Business and professional email.  
**Month 2:** publish helpful educational content (Tamil and English initially), such as what birth details are needed for a Jathagam reading, what Porutham means, and how a consultation works.  
**Month 3:** request voluntary feedback from actual clients; review enquiry-to-booking conversion, no-show rate, repeat enquiries and client satisfaction. Improve services based on evidence.

### Content pillars
- Vedic astrology fundamentals explained simply.
- Tamil Jothidam terms and traditions.
- How to prepare for a consultation.
- Panchangam and Muhurtham education, with dates checked against a defined source.
- Responsible guidance: uncertainty, personal agency and avoiding fear-based claims.

### Channels

- WhatsApp Business catalogue/profile and opt-in updates.
- Instagram / Facebook educational posts.
- YouTube short explainers, if Abirami is comfortable appearing on camera.
- Referrals and community networks, with no pressure or misleading claims.
